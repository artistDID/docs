# Decentralized identity for artists.

**artistDID** is an open-source initiative to build a decentralized identity infrastructure for artists, enabling them to establish, own, manage, and prove their identity independently of platforms, institutions, and intermediaries.

We believe artists should have sovereign control over their digital identity, their professional presence, and the credentials that attest to their work and achievements.

artistDID builds on decentralized identifiers (DIDs), public-key cryptography, and content-addressed storage to provide an open, portable, and verifiable foundation for artistic identity.

**Website:** [https://artistdid.org](https://artistdid.org)

---

## Why artistDID?

An artist's digital identity is often fragmented across social networks, streaming services, marketplaces, institutional databases, and other centralized platforms.

These platforms control how identities are represented, verified, discovered, and accessed. An artist's professional presence can become dependent on services that they neither own nor control.

We believe there should be a better foundation.

An artist should have a persistent digital identity that:

- **Belongs to the artist.** Identity is created and controlled by the artist, not granted by a platform.
- **Is independently verifiable.** Anyone can verify identity records and cryptographic proofs without relying on a centralized authority.
- **Is portable.** Identity and its associated information can be used across different platforms, applications, and services.
- **Is resilient.** Information can be published through decentralized, content-addressed infrastructure rather than depending on a single provider.
- **Is open.** The underlying protocols, specifications, and reference implementations are publicly available.

artistDID aims to provide the infrastructure that makes this possible.

### The importance of artistic identity in the AI era

As artificial intelligence transforms how art is created, distributed, and experienced, the ability to establish the origin, authorship, and authenticity of creative work becomes increasingly important. In a world where images, music, voices, and entire artistic identities can be generated or replicated by AI, artists need a reliable way to establish who they are and provide verifiable connections to their work, history, and creative contributions. artistDID provides an open, decentralized foundation for this purpose, allowing artists to maintain cryptographic ownership of their identities and enabling others to independently verify identity claims and attestations. Rather than attempting to determine what is human-made or AI-generated, artistDID focuses on something more fundamental: ensuring that artistic identity, authorship claims, and the relationships between artists and their work can be established and verified independently of platforms and intermediaries.

## Design principles

artistDID is guided by a few fundamental principles:

**Self-sovereignty**

Artists should own and control their identities and cryptographic keys.

**Decentralization**

No single organization, platform, or service should be required to create, resolve, or verify an artist's identity.

**Verifiability**

Trust should be established through cryptographic proofs and independently verifiable data, not through the authority of a centralized platform.

**Interoperability**

The system should be based on open standards and extensible data models, making integration with other identity systems and creative platforms possible.

**Longevity**

Artist identity should outlive individual platforms, applications, institutions, and service providers.

**Open source**

The specifications and reference implementations should be openly developed and available for anyone to inspect, implement, and improve.

## Open development

artistDID is an open-source project. Its specifications, protocols, reference implementations, and supporting tools are developed with the goal of creating an accessible public infrastructure for artistic identity.

We welcome contributions, technical discussions, research, and collaboration from developers, artists, cultural organizations, and anyone interested in the future of decentralized identity.
